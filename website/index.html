What it does

Everything a training log and a food diary are supposed to do, plus the parts that usually get left to guesswork.

Nutrition

Log food without fighting it

Scan a barcode, photograph a nutrition label, describe a meal in words, or pick it off your campus dining hall's live menu. Portions rescale properly, and a value the source never reported stays blank instead of quietly becoming zero.

Training

The split you actually run

Build routines with supersets, rest and rep ranges saved in, then log the session as it happens — swap an exercise when the rack is busy, add one that was never in the plan, and fix any of it afterwards. 230+ exercises, or add your own.

Volume

Hard sets, counted honestly

Weekly volume per muscle, where assisting work is credited separately from direct work so a pressing-heavy week does not read as triceps overtraining. Warm-ups are logged and never counted. Drop sets and sets to failure are.

Progress

Signal, not scale noise

A smoothed weight trend that separates real change from water and food, energy expenditure estimated from your own adherent days, and progress photos that stay on your phone and are never uploaded anywhere.

Beyond weights

Planks, carries and treadmills

Sets can be weight and reps, bodyweight reps, a duration or a distance — because a plank is not three reps of anything. Photograph a treadmill console and it reads the display, adding the steps a phone left on the rail never counted.

Platform

Built for the phone in your pocket

Apple Health in both directions, your day on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island, and a diary that works with no signal in a basement gym and syncs when it can. Thirteen languages, right-to-left included.

How it counts

Most of the decisions in this app are about refusing to make a number up. A few of them, plainly:

Missing is not zero A micronutrient the database never reported shows as unreported, not as none.
An estimate says so Steps worked out from stride length are labelled an estimate everywhere they appear, including in the database.
Body mass is not load An unweighted pull-up moves no external weight, so it adds none to your volume. The plates on the belt do.
A scan is verified Two camera frames have to agree, and the barcode's own check digit has to pass, before anything is looked up.
Your photos stay put Progress photos never leave the device. That is a deliberate limit, not a feature gap.
Nothing invented into Health Apple Health records what devices measured. CalCamp never writes an estimate into it.
