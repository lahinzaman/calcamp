Eating on a meal plan, tracked properly

Tracking calories at university is annoying for one specific reason: you did not cook the food and there is no package to read. So everyone ends up searching "chicken" and picking whichever result feels about right.

  • ✓Today's real menu, by dining hall. Pull up what is on the line right now and tap the thing you actually took.
  • ✓Every meal period. Breakfast, lunch, dinner and late-night takeout, each with their own menu.
  • ✓Nutrition from the hall itself. Calories and macros as your university publishes them, not a stranger's guess.
  • ✓Find what fits what is left. Short on protein with 500 calories to spend? It will tell you what on the menu works.
  • ✓Off campus too. Scan a barcode, photograph a nutrition label, or just describe what you ate.

Launching with Rutgers–New Brunswick dining halls, with more universities to follow.

Everything else it does

A food diary and a training log that work the way you actually eat and train.

Food

Log a meal in seconds

Scan a barcode, snap the nutrition label, pick it off the dining hall menu, or type what you ate in plain English. Your usual meals come back as one tap next time.

Training

The gym, handled

Build your split once, then log it as you lift. Rest timer, supersets, plate maths, and what you lifted last time so you know what to beat.

Progress

See past the daily noise

The scale jumps for water and salt, not fat. CalCamp draws the line underneath it, so you can tell a real change from a bad Tuesday.

Targets

Numbers that fit you

Your calorie and protein targets come from your own weigh-ins and what you actually ate, and they adjust as you go. Not a generic calculator.

Cardio

Treadmills and walks count

Photograph the treadmill console and it reads the display. Steps, workouts and active calories sync with Apple Health both ways.

Everyday

Made for a real day

Your calories live on the Lock Screen. It works with no signal in a basement gym and catches up later. Thirteen languages.

Why you can trust the numbers

A tracker is only worth the effort if the figures are honest. A few rules CalCamp sticks to:

It never makes a number up If the dining hall did not publish the sodium, you see a dash — not a zero that quietly drags your day's total down.
A guess is labelled a guess When steps are worked out from your stride instead of counted, it says so, and you can correct it.
Warm-ups are not working sets Your training volume counts the sets that actually cost you something.
Scans get double-checked A barcode has to read the same twice and pass its own checksum before anything reaches your day.
Your photos never leave your phone Progress photos are stored on the device and are never uploaded anywhere.
You can read the code CalCamp is open source. If you want to know exactly what it does with your data, you can go and look.
